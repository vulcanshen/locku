# Changelog

## [Unreleased]
### Changed
- `q` and `Ctrl-C` leave the settings screen the same way, from anywhere but a box being typed in: with colours unsaved they ask first, and a second `Ctrl-C` while that asks leaves at once. `Ctrl-C` used to leave at once, unsaved colours and all, and `q` did nothing while a menu or a popup was open.
- `Space` opens and closes the Space menu and nothing else: it no longer cancels a confirm or closes an options list.
- Every Space menu ends in a third region, `global operation`, with `Quit`; a rule parts one region from the next.
- `?` on a panel is a menu: `Quit`, which runs from there, over a reference of the keys. On a popup it is that popup's own keys. On preference's, tmux's and screen's `[2]` it is still what each row means.
- A row that cannot run right now is dimmed and keeps its own description; pressing it does nothing, where it used to say why in a toast.
- `P`, the preview, is a row of the Space menu on preference's, tmux's and screen's `[2]` too.

### Fixed
- The first `Ctrl-C` on the splash only closes it, as any other key does, rather than leaving.

## [0.1.1] - 2026-09-26
### Changed
- The splash behind `V` is now the icon itself, cell for cell: the navy U around the gold letters of LOCK, each wrapping the next, revealed from the outside in before the U rises around them. It used to show a padlock the icon never had.

## [0.1.0] - 2026-09-25
### Added
- `locku lock`: the terminal as one LED board — every cell a Nerd Font square, dark in the saver's `bg`, lit in its `fg` — spelling the clock in a right-angled 3 × 7 pixel font, the look of a seven-segment display (no slash in the zero, no diagonal anywhere; M and W five wide, the space two, the hyphen three), at the saver's `size` (small, medium, large: one font pixel is 1, 2 or 3 cells square); what does not fit sheds the year, the seconds, then the date before the size steps down. A change lands as a shuffled reveal of only the pixels that differ.
- A second saver type, `dino`: the offline dinosaur game as a screensaver. The ground and the obstacles scroll by — cacti or pyramids, in three sizes, small, medium and large — and the T-Rex jumps them by itself — as high as each asks, a low jump over a small one, a middling one over a medium, the high one over a large; at a random moment inside the window that clears them, and now and then for nothing, at any of the three heights — for ever, never dying; no score, no clock. Its settings are `runner` — `big` or `small`, one T-Rex of that size, or two one behind the other: `big-big`, `small-small`, `small-big` or `big-small`, the name read left to right as they stand on the screen, each jumping on its own; a file's `trex` or `two-trex` from before is read as `big` or `big-small` and saved as that — `scene` (`grassland` with its cacti, or `desert` with pyramids) and the two colours — no size: it is drawn as large as the terminal allows; fourteen frames a second, replaced whole. Each saver's keys are its own in the file: a dino profile has no shapes, a clock profile no runner.
- A third saver type, `custom`: a program of your own, `command`, run through `sh -c` on a pty of locku's, its output passed on to the terminal as it comes — `cmatrix -b`, say. It is a process of its own and nothing more: not restarted, not read, told its size as any terminal would, and killed with its whole process group when the lock ends; keys never reach it. A key puts the PIN prompt over its picture, which goes on moving: every chunk the program writes is followed by the box again, the cursor and its attributes saved and restored around it, inside one synchronised update, so nothing it draws is dropped and the box is always on top; when the prompt closes its place is blanked, and only a program that has sat still for half a second is asked to paint itself — one that draws fills the place by itself, and asked would start its picture over (measured with cmatrix). The right PIN ends everything. A program that ends is not restarted: the lock goes on with the word for it on locku's own board — `EXIT` and its code, as a shell would report it — EXIT in the default gold, the code green for 0 and peach for the rest, a signal counted as 128 and its number — or `NONE`, red, when nothing ran, and the reason in red on the status row. It has no colours of its own. Its preview from the settings screen — `p` on the row, `P` on its [2] or, as the active profile, on any other [2] — hands the terminal to the program until a key.
- The time is `HH MM` or `HH MM SS`, twenty-four hours, its groups parted by a space rather than a colon; the date keeps its hyphens.
- A saver's `font`: `3x7`, or `3x5` — the same right-angled alphabet five rows tall, so a column of `HH` / `MM` / `SS` at large fits 54 rows instead of 72.
- The time and the date are laid out as two blocks, each on its own: the time first, in the whole canvas; the date in what is left — under the time in the row layout, to its left in the column layout. Each keeps its size while any of its content fits, stepping the size down before dropping a unit (the seconds, the year); a date that fits at nothing is left out.
- The gap is its own unit: the dark between two glyphs, between two lines, and the space between two groups — one cell at small and medium, two at large — rather than a font pixel scaled with the digits. Large `HH MM SS` needs 148 columns rather than 178; the time and the date sit two gap units apart under each other, six beside.
- The R, G and B sliders wear their channel's colour at its value, on a ground that runs the other way — white at 0, black at 255.
- A saver's `layout`: `row` (the time on one line, the date on the next) or `column` (`HH` over `MM` over `SS`, the date's parts under them), which makes the digits several times bigger.
- The board goes on ticking under the PIN prompt, dimmed; the prompt is 48 columns with a row of air above and below, and the dots — one a character, a space between — grow out from its middle. The settings screen's PIN boxes are drawn the same way.
- Any key raises the PIN prompt; the key itself is not input. `Enter` unlocks, `Esc` goes back; a wrong PIN holds the prompt red for a second; `wrong_pin_attempts` wrong PINs in a row start a `wrong_pin_attempt_cooldown` countdown; `pin_prompt_timeout` seconds of silence close the prompt. How many idle seconds before tmux or screen locks by itself is each tool's own row, under the tool's own name for it — tmux's `lock-after-time`, screen's `idle` — which activate writes as it is.
- `locku pin reset`, the way back from a forgotten PIN, from any shell of your own: y/N, then your login password — the account is the one boundary locku has — then a new eight-digit PIN, made and shown once and written over the old, the way elasticsearch resets a password; the reset is logged under `~/.locku/data`, without the PIN. A lock already up reads the file's PIN at every key, so the new PIN opens it at once and the old one no longer does.
- Without a PIN the lock is a screensaver: any key ends it, and the status row says so. A config file that cannot be read fails open the same way.
- The status row: `user@host · locked since HH:MM`, `show_status: false` to hide it.
- Signals are ignored, the tty is held raw, a panic puts the lock back up; the process ends only for the right PIN, any key with no PIN, or the terminal going away.
- `locku`: the settings screen. Savers are the kinds — the clock, the dino, the custom program — each with its defaults (the clock large, in the short face, with seconds and the full date; the dino its first runner and scene; the custom program none), editable in [2] for the profiles made of it from then on, `p` previewing them and `n` making a profile of them; profiles are the named, configured ones: a profile's shapes and its two colours by RGB sliders (a draft until `S` saves it, `R` drops it, `q` asks when one is unsaved), duplicated, renamed, deleted, previewed one by one with `p`; preference: the PIN (set; once set, the current one opens the choice of a new one or none), the active profile, `show_status`, `pin_prompt_timeout`, `wrong_pin_attempts`, `wrong_pin_attempt_cooldown`; `?` on their panel is their glossary alone, wrapped, and the keys elsewhere. Everything but the colours is written at once. `P` on a [2] previews the lock in place; on [1] it does nothing, `p` previewing the row there.
- On [1], `a` on a profile makes it the one the lock shows, at once — the dot moves, the file is written; preference › profile stays the other way to the same setting.
- Every [2] is a table under a header row, Property and Value, and every panel's title is a capsule the way the family draws them, flush after the corner: the panel and its name in the border's colour, `unsaved` chained after it in yellow while the panel has the keys. The panels loop: `k` on the first row is the last, `j` on the last the first, as in the menus.
- The file names them the same way: `profile` is the active one, `profiles` the list, each profile's `saver` its kind, and `savers` each kind's defaults; the earlier `saver`, a `savers` list and `type` are read and carried over.
- Integration, the sidebar's third group: `tmux` and `screen`, each with the file to write, `config file path`, and its idle time under the tool's own name, `lock-after-time` or `idle`; tmux has a `lock` — `lock-server` or `lock-session` — and a `bind-key` too, the key after prefix that locks every client as tmux spells it — `l`, `C-l` — or empty for none; screen has a `bind`, the key after C-a that locks as screen's bind spells it — `l`, `^L` — or empty for none, on top of screen's own `C-a x`, which locks anyway. The first row of the tool's [2] is `activate`, on while locku's managed block is in that file and off while it is not: Enter turns it, after a confirm (on applies to a running tmux server, or to every running screen, too; for screen, `LOCKPRG` goes into the shell rc as well), and while it is on every row is written into the file the moment it changes — a `config file path` that moves takes the block along — until off takes it out, off a running tmux server or the running screens included; with no file set activate is disabled and says so rather than guess where the file is. A rule parts locku's two rows from the tool's own keys under them. Every line of a block ends in `# locku`; `?` on that panel explains its rows. (This was a `locku setup [-d]` command for a day, `S` / `X` hotkeys for a morning and a button for an afternoon.)
- The tmux block binds no key unless `bind-key` names one — a tmux user has keys of their own — but a command alias: `prefix :` then `locku` is `lock-server`, every client on the server — or, with `lock` set to `lock-session`, this session's clients alone, the other sessions left as they are: the mark goes on the session, the same hooks read it, and each session's own lock-command tells its lock which session it is; a running server takes the block whole — the same lines the file got, sourced from a file of their own — with what the old block did and the new one does not undone first (a key unbound, a lock switched clearing every mark and the session hook), and each session's own lock-command set by hand, since the hook reaches only the sessions made from then on; a key named is so bound on a running server too, and unbound when it is emptied or when activate goes off. And the server stays locked for whoever comes: `locku lock` marks it `@locked` while it runs, two hooks lock a client that attaches to or switches into any session while the mark is up, and the mark goes when a PIN unlocks — not when a terminal dies under the lock. The idle lock is tmux's own, a session at a time. The lock command is locku by its absolute path — the client's shell need not have it on PATH — told the server's socket (`locku lock -S`), so a server on another socket works too. `make e2e` runs it all on a real tmux.
- The screen block is the tmux block's shape under screen's own names: `idle N lockscreen`, and `bind <key> lockscreen` when `bind` names a key — none by default, since `C-a x` is screen's — every line marked `# locku`; LOCKPRG, the lock itself, goes into the shell rc, because screen reads it from the shell that started it and never from the screenrc. A screen already running takes the idle time and the key at once — `screen -X` to every session `screen -ls` lists, best effort — and loses them when activate goes off; its LOCKPRG is the shell's it was attached from, so until it is detached and attached again from a new shell it locks with screen's own lock, which the toast and `?` say. There is no `lock` for screen: a screen is a process of its own, with no server to scope a lock to. `make e2e` runs the screen side on a real screen too.
- A preview from the settings screen is a look at the saver, not the lock: any key comes back, PIN or no PIN.
- A tool's `config file path` is typed on an offer the way webu's settings are: the value, or the usual file, shown dim; `Tab` takes it, `Backspace` declines it, `Enter` on an untouched offer changes nothing.
- `SCREEN-LOCK` as argv[0] runs the lock, which is how LOCKPRG is called by screen.
- `docs/icon.svg`: the family's block-letter mark on a black square, locku's own.
- macOS and Linux (WSL too); Windows is not supported: the lock rests on the tty, the pty, `su` and tmux / screen, and a native port would be another product.
- `make check` runs the tests under the race detector: three packages hold goroutines now — the custom saver's pump and screen, the login check, the lock's prompt — and a race would not show in a plain run.
