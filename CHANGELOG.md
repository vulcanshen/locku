# Changelog

## [Unreleased]
### Added
- `locku lock`: the terminal as one LED board — every cell a Nerd Font square, dark in the saver's `bg`, lit in its `fg` — spelling the clock in a right-angled 3 × 7 pixel font, the look of a seven-segment display (no slash in the zero, no diagonal anywhere; M and W five wide, the space two, the hyphen three), at the saver's `size` (small, medium, large: one font pixel is 1, 2 or 3 cells square); what does not fit sheds the year, the seconds, then the date before the size steps down. A change lands as a shuffled reveal of only the pixels that differ.
- A second saver type, `dino`: the offline dinosaur game as a screensaver. The ground and the cacti scroll by, the T-Rex jumps them by itself — at a random moment inside the window that clears them, and now and then for nothing — for ever, never dying; no score, no clock. Its settings are `runner` (`trex`, or `two-trex`: a small one in front of the big one, each jumping on its own), `scene` (`grassland` with its cacti, or `desert` with pyramids) and the two colours — no size: it is drawn as large as the terminal allows; fourteen frames a second, replaced whole. Each saver's keys are its own in the file: a dino profile has no shapes, a clock profile no runner.
- The time is `HH MM` or `HH MM SS`, twenty-four hours, its groups parted by a space rather than a colon; the date keeps its hyphens.
- A saver's `font`: `3x7`, or `3x5` — the same right-angled alphabet five rows tall, so a column of `HH` / `MM` / `SS` at large fits 54 rows instead of 72.
- The time and the date are laid out as two blocks, each on its own: the time first, in the whole canvas; the date in what is left — under the time in the row layout, to its left in the column layout. Each keeps its size while any of its content fits, stepping the size down before dropping a unit (the seconds, the year); a date that fits at nothing is left out.
- The gap is its own unit: the dark between two glyphs, between two lines, and the space between two groups — one cell at small and medium, two at large — rather than a font pixel scaled with the digits. Large `HH MM SS` needs 148 columns rather than 178; the time and the date sit two gap units apart under each other, six beside.
- The R, G and B sliders wear their channel's colour at its value, on a ground that runs the other way — white at 0, black at 255.
- A saver's `layout`: `row` (the time on one line, the date on the next) or `column` (`HH` over `MM` over `SS`, the date's parts under them), which makes the digits several times bigger.
- The board goes on ticking under the PIN prompt, dimmed; the prompt is 48 columns with a row of air above and below, and the dots — one a character, a space between — grow out from its middle. The settings screen's PIN boxes are drawn the same way.
- Any key raises the PIN prompt; the key itself is not input. `Enter` unlocks, `Esc` goes back; a wrong PIN holds the prompt red for a second; `wrong_pin_attempts` wrong PINs in a row start a `wrong_pin_attempt_cooldown` countdown; `pin_prompt_timeout` seconds of silence close the prompt. `idle_lock` is how many idle seconds before tmux or screen lock by themselves — one value, which `locku setup` hands to both.
- Without a PIN the lock is a screensaver: any key ends it, and the status row says so. A config file that cannot be read fails open the same way.
- The status row: `user@host · locked since HH:MM`, `show_status: false` to hide it.
- Signals are ignored, the tty is held raw, a panic puts the lock back up; the process ends only for the right PIN, any key with no PIN, or the terminal going away.
- `locku`: the settings screen. Savers are the kinds — the clock, the dino — each with its defaults (the clock large, in the short face, with seconds and the full date; the dino its first runner and scene), editable in [2] for the profiles made of it from then on, `p` previewing them and `n` making a profile of them; profiles are the named, configured ones: a profile's shapes and its two colours by RGB sliders (a draft until `S` saves it, `R` drops it, `q` asks when one is unsaved), duplicated, renamed, deleted, previewed one by one with `p`; preference: the PIN (set; once set, the current one opens the choice of a new one or none), the active profile, `show_status`, `pin_prompt_timeout`, `wrong_pin_attempts`, `wrong_pin_attempt_cooldown`, `idle_lock`. Everything but the colours is written at once. `P` previews the lock in place.
- Every preference row has a dim line under it saying what it is. The panels loop: `k` on the first row is the last, `j` on the last the first, as in the menus.
- The file names them the same way: `profile` is the active one, `profiles` the list, each profile's `saver` its kind, and `savers` each kind's defaults; the earlier `saver`, a `savers` list and `type` are read and carried over.
- `locku setup [tmux|screen]`: a managed block in the file preference names as `tmux_conf` (applied to a running server too) and in the one named as `screen_conf`, plus `LOCKPRG` in the shell rc. With either unset, setup says so and writes nothing rather than guessing where the file is. Every line of a block ends in `# locku`; `locku setup -d [tmux|screen]` takes the blocks out again, and off a running tmux server.
- The tmux block binds no key — a tmux user has keys of their own — but a command alias: `prefix :` then `locku` is `lock-session`. And a locked session stays locked for whoever comes: `locku lock` marks the session `@locked` while it runs, two hooks lock a client that attaches to or switches into a marked session, and the mark goes when a PIN unlocks — not when a terminal dies under the lock. The lock command is locku by its absolute path — the client's shell need not have it on PATH — told the server's socket (`locku lock -S`), so a server on another socket works too. `make e2e` runs it all on a real tmux.
- Preference has `tmux_conf` and `screen_conf`, typed on an offer the way webu's settings are: the value, or the usual file, shown dim; `Tab` takes it, `Backspace` declines it, `Enter` on an untouched offer changes nothing.
- `SCREEN-LOCK` as argv[0] runs the lock, which is how LOCKPRG is called by screen.
